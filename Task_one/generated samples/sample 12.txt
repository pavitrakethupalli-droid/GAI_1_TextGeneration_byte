# Critical Thinking: Evaluating Conflicting Answers from AI Assistants

## 1. Introduction

Artificial Intelligence (AI) assistants are widely used by students to understand concepts, solve problems, and prepare assignments. However, sometimes two AI assistants provide different or conflicting answers to the same question.

This does not necessarily mean that both answers are wrong. The difference may arise from how the models were trained, how they interpret the question, or the information they use. Therefore, students should develop critical-thinking skills to evaluate AI-generated information before accepting it as correct.

## 2. Why Might Different AI Models Produce Different Responses?

Several factors can cause AI assistants to generate different answers.

1\. Different Training Data: AI models are trained on different collections of text, code, and other information. Their knowledge and examples may therefore differ.

2\. Different Model Designs: Models may use different architectures, training methods, and reasoning approaches, leading to different interpretations of a question.

3\. Ambiguous Questions: If a question is unclear or has multiple meanings, each assistant may interpret it differently.

4\. Different Knowledge Cut-off Dates: Models may have different limits on the information included during training. For questions about recent events, one answer may be outdated.

5\. Probabilistic Responses: AI models generate responses based on learned patterns. Depending on their settings and context, they may produce different explanations or conclusions even when given the same prompt.

6\. Errors and Hallucinations: An AI assistant may confidently produce incorrect facts, calculations, references, or explanations. A fluent answer is not automatically a correct answer.

For example, two AI assistants may provide different answers to a programming question because one overlooks an edge case or assumes a different programming language version.

## 3. A Systematic Method for Checking Both Answers

A student should follow a step-by-step verification process rather than choosing the answer that sounds more convincing.

Step 1: Understand the Question

Identify exactly what is being asked and clarify any ambiguous terms, assumptions, or conditions.

Step 2: Compare Both Answers

Break each response into individual claims. Identify the points where they agree, disagree, or make different assumptions.

Step 3: Consult Reliable Sources

Check textbooks, official documentation, academic papers, government websites, or other trustworthy sources relevant to the subject.

Step 4: Test or Verify the Claims

Recalculate mathematical results, run code in a safe test environment, examine original research, or use a suitable experiment.

Step 5: Reach an Evidence-Based Conclusion

Accept the answer best supported by evidence. If the evidence is insufficient or conflicting, acknowledge the uncertainty instead of guessing.

### Example

Suppose two AI assistants give different answers to the question, “What is the time complexity of binary search?”

- Assistant A: \\(O(\log n)\\)
- Assistant B: \\(O(n)\\)

The student should check an algorithms textbook or reliable course material. Binary search repeatedly halves a sorted search space, so its usual worst-case time complexity is \\(O(\log n)\\). A linear search, which may examine each element, has \\(O(n)\\) worst-case time complexity.

This verification process helps the student identify which answer is correct and understand why the other answer is incorrect under the stated conditions.

## 4. How Reliable Sources and Evidence Support Conclusions

Reliable sources provide a factual foundation for evaluating AI responses. They help students distinguish supported information from unsupported claims.

Important ways to use evidence include:

- Textbooks: Useful for established concepts, definitions, formulas, and theories.
- Official documentation: Provides authoritative details about programming languages, software, and technical tools.
- Peer-reviewed research: Helps evaluate scientific findings and research-based claims.
- Government and institutional websites: Useful for official statistics, regulations, and public information.
- Experiments and calculations: Allow students to test whether a claim works in practice.
- Multiple independent sources: Help determine whether a conclusion is supported beyond a single website or AI assistant.

Students should also evaluate the author's expertise, publication date, evidence, possible bias, and whether the source directly supports the specific claim.

Importantly, using several sources is not enough if they all repeat the same original error. Students should seek original evidence where possible and distinguish between facts, interpretations, and opinions.

## 5. Recommendations for Responsible AI Use

Students can use AI responsibly by following these recommendations:

1. Verify important information: Do not automatically accept AI-generated answers, especially for academic, medical, financial, or safety-related decisions.
2. Use AI as a learning assistant: Ask for explanations, examples, and feedback rather than relying entirely on generated answers.
3. Check references: Confirm that cited books, papers, websites, and quotations actually exist and support the claims.
4. Protect personal information: Avoid sharing passwords, private records, or confidential academic and institutional data.
5. Avoid plagiarism: Use AI according to institutional rules, acknowledge its assistance when required, and write assignments in your own understanding.
6. Recognise limitations: AI can make mistakes, reflect biases, or provide outdated information.
7. Maintain independent thinking: Question assumptions, compare evidence, and form conclusions through reasoning rather than popularity or confidence.
8. Acknowledge uncertainty: When reliable evidence does not settle a disagreement, explain what remains unknown and seek expert guidance when appropriate.

## 6. Conclusion

Different AI assistants may provide conflicting responses because of differences in training data, model design, question interpretation, and the possibility of errors. Students should not choose an answer simply because it is longer, more confident, or easier to understand.

Instead, they should compare claims systematically, consult reliable sources, test results where possible, and draw conclusions supported by evidence. Responsible AI use combines the speed and convenience of artificial intelligence with human judgement, curiosity, and critical thinking.

The key lesson is that AI can assist the learning process, but evidence and independent reasoning should guide the final conclusion.
